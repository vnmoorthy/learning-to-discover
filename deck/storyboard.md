### Likely questions, crisp answers

**Did you beat a record?**

Not yet, and we say so on the slide. Status as of Nov 15, 8:47 AM PT: no record beaten yet; closest: no-three-in-line, 150 points, 116 violated lines left. We also have a proved negative result on Sidon (4,5)-sets. Everything we do claim went through the official verifier.

**What is new compared with AlphaEvolve or Karpathy's autoresearch?**

Three things. One: every proposal carries a falsifiable prediction, and a critic scores it later, so the lab knows when its own reasoning was wrong. Two: two models from different labs must agree before compute moves, with a deterministic planner under both. Three: the lab improves its own parts. It fine-tunes a model on verifier reward, writes new search programs, and commissions new tools.

**How do we know the results are real?**

The search never grades itself. The critic runs the official EinsteinArena verifier in a separate process. It has run 14 times in this log. You can press Verify now in the app and watch it.

**How autonomous is it? What did humans do?**

We wrote the lab, the first search scripts and the verifier harness, and we started the first searches by hand. The loop adopted those processes. After that it decides what runs, what stops and where compute goes. In this run that is 16 cycles and 306 logged decisions.

**Does it really improve itself?**

Two ways, both measured. The Tinker run: valid constructions on held-out prompts went from 0% to 50%, mean reward from 0.144 to 0.525. And the prediction scores: 3 confirmed, 3 refuted, 2 pending. Each scored prediction becomes a lesson in memory that the next proposal reads. The trained model has not beaten a record. The claim is the curve.

**Why a referee model?**

One model proposing and judging its own ideas is the failure we wanted to avoid. Claude Fable sees the live evidence and each proposal, and can approve, veto or amend. In this log: 28 abstain, 17 approve. If the referee fails, it abstains and the planner's hard limits still apply.

**past.dev versus markdown and RAG?**

We measured it on real lab facts. On 31 questions over 26 real lab facts, markdown is the most accurate (100%); past.dev scores 74%; past.dev is cheapest per question and we report that as measured. past.dev holds the shared lab memory that agents read from each other; the markdown file and the small RAG index exist only as baselines.

**Where is Exa in the loop?**

In the main loop, as the literature step. The proposer writes the queries, Exa returns sources, and decisions cite them. 155 sources stored, 27 cited by decisions. Decision 294 is an example.

**What does Modal do?**

Two jobs. Sandboxes: 22 AI-written search programs ran in parallel with no network and no secrets, and 22 returned a solution the verifier accepted. And an elastic CPU fleet: 113 containers launched by the planner.

**What about Devin?**

Devin is the toolsmith. 8 tools commissioned, 0 adopted after passing the verifier in a Modal sandbox, 9 rejected.

**What failed?**

Sidon (4,5)-sets: exact search proved there is no small extension of the record set. 3 searches are reported as stalled. The planner rejected 7 proposals. The loop stopped 29 runs. All of that is in the log.

**Why these problems?**

They are open EinsteinArena problems with exact public verifiers and live leaderboards, so a claim is either valid or it is not. No-three-in-line on the 75 grid is the last open case up to 76.
